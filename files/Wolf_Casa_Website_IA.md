# Wolf Casa — Website Information Architecture
### From product catalogue to composed-living house
Prepared from: *Wolf Casa × Hue Cycle Labs — Way of Light & Form, Vol. I 2026* and *The Wolf Casa Way — 90-Day Playbook*

---

## 1. The gap this closes

The current site (wolfcasa.nowfloats.com) is a templated product-catalogue: an "all-products" grid, SKU-style categories, no editorial voice, no worlds, no trade path, no consultation funnel. The brand deck's own diagnosis is the brief: *"Wolf Casa already holds the substance... What it needs is language, hierarchy, and a world worth entering."*

This IA is built to do three things a catalogue site can't:
1. Sell **compositions, not SKUs** — every path leads through a room, not a product grid.
2. Give the brand's five-layer IP system (Master → World → Composition → Object → Experience) a literal home in the nav.
3. Open a **trade/specifier door** and a **consultation funnel** as first-class journeys, not afterthoughts.

**One naming note to confirm before build:** the brand deck and the 90-day plan use two different names for the same middle layer of the hierarchy — "Worlds" (Heirloom Way, Low House Way...) in the master deck vs. mood-based "Compositions" (Nocturne Den, Golden Prowl...) in the playbook. This IA treats them as three nested levels — **World → Composition/Room → Named Object** — rather than competing systems. Flag this to the brand team; the IA below assumes that reading is correct.

## 3. Section-by-section breakdown

### 3.1 Home
**Purpose:** Prove "composed, not decorated" in the first screen — no product grid above the fold.
- Hero: manifesto line + signature film/imagery ("A home is not furnished. It is composed.")
- The positioning statement (short form)
- Entry points into the 5 Worlds (visual, not a dropdown list)
- One current Composition spotlight (e.g. Golden Prowl) with named objects inside it
- Furniture Tourism teaser (the signature offer)
- The Journal — latest 2–3 pieces
- Signature Reveal — most recent completed home
- Consultation CTA + trust markers (15 years, 40k sq ft, 110+ people)

### 3.2 Way of Light & Form (Philosophy)
**Purpose:** The brand doctrine — this is what makes the site a "house," not a shop.
- Master philosophy: Way / Light / Form, explained
- The Manifesto (full, as a standalone reading page)
- Quiet Signatures — the brand-expression voice
- The House of Modern Indian Living — market position
- Curators of a Life Well Lived — service philosophy
- The positioning statement, in full

### 3.3 The Wolf Way (the core "shop" — three nested levels)
This is the section that replaces the old product catalogue. Structure:

**Level 1 — The Worlds (landing page: all 5, as doors not tiles)**
- The Heirloom Way · The Low House Way · The Courtyard Way · The Monastic Way · The Ritual Way
- Each World page: philosophy paragraph, the "for..." use-case line, mood imagery, then its Compositions

**Level 2 — Compositions (mood-based rooms within a World)**
- e.g. under Heirloom Way: Golden Prowl (hosted dining), Artisan Mark (collector layer)
- Each Composition page: the light/form description, category list, a short editorial line, and the named objects that make it up
- Cross-linked by the "Three Lenses" (Shadow / Material / Object) as a secondary filter, not a nav item

**Level 3 — Named Objects (the actual products, edited as heirlooms, not SKUs)**
- The Low Sofa, The Hearth Kitchen, The Heirloom Table, The Monastic Bed, The Shadow Lamp, The Quiet Wardrobe, The Ground Rug, The Still Chair, The Stone Table, The Still Bath, The Courtyard Kitchen, The Object Cabinet
- Each object page: the editorial name and one-line philosophy, then practical specs (materials, dimensions, lead time) — practical detail is present but never leads

**Utility layer within this section:**
- A **composition finder** ("find your Wolf Way") — the quiz/poll mechanism from the playbook, doubling as the Room Audit lead tool
- Filters by category (sofa, kitchen, wardrobe...) for people who arrive with a specific need — but filters are secondary to the Worlds/Compositions browse path, never the entry point

### 3.4 Experiences
**Purpose:** Sell the six signature rituals as destinations, not services buried in an "About" page.
1. **Signature Consultation** — the design diagnosis (booking flow lives here)
2. **The Materials Library** — tactile, with a content series pulled from the Journal
3. **Furniture Tourism / Casa Sojourn** — its own sub-site-like page: the Foshan journey, day-by-day itinerary, 11-step sourcing pipeline, and an application form (this is the highest-value lead path in the whole site)
4. **The Signature Reveal** — a running gallery of completed-home reveal films
5. **Signature Evenings** — invite/waitlist page
6. **The Signature Circle** — the community layer (could start as a simple registry/newsletter, expand later)

### 3.5 The Journal
**Purpose:** The "teach taste before selling product" engine — this is what makes Wolf Casa a category authority, not a store.
- Editorial articles organised by the 9 content pillars (Way of Light, Way of Form, Furniture Tourism, The Kitchen, The Wardrobe, Lived Emotion, Curation, Found Luxury, Material Intelligence)
- Knowledge Unplugged — the education series (light, sourcing, composition explainers)
- The Podcast — episode archive, embeds
- Press/design-media features

### 3.6 The House
**Purpose:** Legacy and trust — "a brand is trusted through its legacy, its people, and the face that stands behind it."
- Legacy numbers (15 years, 40k sq ft, 3k sq ft flagship, 110+ people)
- International sourcing story (Foshan, Milan, Jaipur craft)
- The Directors (founder-led, four roles)
- The Foundation — craft patronage and apprenticeship
- Retail formats: The Atelier, The Boutique, The Experience Showroom (also referenced from Visit)
- The horizon/ambition statement (useful for press, investors, trade partners — lower priority for consumer nav but should exist)

### 3.7 Trade (Specifier Desk)
**Purpose:** A distinct door for architects, designers, and builders — currently has no home on the catalogue site at all.
- Why partner with Wolf Casa (the "Architects of Atmosphere" positioning)
- Login/account request for catalogues, samples, quotation requests, project basket (per the playbook's Builder & Specifier Web App plan — can launch as a request-access form before the full portal is built)
- Case studies of architect-led projects
- Direct contact path, separate from consumer WhatsApp CTA

### 3.8 Visit
**Purpose:** Convert online interest into a showroom visit — the deck is explicit that "social media is the new showroom," so this section has to close that loop.
- Showroom locator (flagship + any franchise locations)
- The three retail formats explained (Atelier / Boutique / Experience Showroom) — helps a visitor pick the right stop
- Hours, directions, Google Business Profile link
- Contact form + WhatsApp + phone

## 5. Suggested build order (mirrors the deck's own 30/90/180/365 roadmap)

1. **Foundation (0–30 days):** Home, Way of Light & Form, The Wolf Way skeleton (5 Worlds + 12 named objects, even before every Composition is fleshed out), Visit, basic Trade request form.
2. **Conversion (30–90 days):** Signature Consultation booking flow, Room Audit/composition-finder tool, Furniture Tourism application page live.
3. **Authority (90–180 days):** The Journal populated, Knowledge Unplugged series, first Signature Reveal gallery entries.
4. **Leadership (180–365 days):** Signature Evenings, Signature Circle, full Specifier/Builder web app, Podcast archive.

## 6. What "good" looks like on comparable sites

Reference-checked against how premium furniture/lifestyle houses structure navigation (Minotti, Roche Bobois, and general luxury-retail UX patterns): category names use brand language, not generic e-commerce terms ("Ready-to-Wear," not "Clothes" — Wolf Casa's equivalent is "The Low Sofa," not "Sofas"); navigation prioritises browsing and mood over search efficiency; imagery is editorial-composed-room, never product-on-white; and the philosophy/story layer sits above the shop layer rather than being buried in an About page. That maps directly onto the structure above — it's largely what the brand deck was already asking for; this IA just gives it URLs and a nav bar.
