Draw the villa as a mansion; plan + compositions beside it

The villa view had been three different things in three attempts (exploded
WebGL slabs, a flat elevation, a narrow gabled tower) and two of them were
live at once on /rooms and /the-house. It is now one shared drawing.

components/house/IsoHouse: a true 30-degree isometric mansion. Wide and low
(facade reads 2.5:1 against wall height, was 1.1:1), under a shallow hipped
roof — a ridge 69% of the building length, hipped back at both ends, rising
31px on screen instead of the 70px gable that read as a lean-to slab. Cornice
and base course carry the classical banding; the portico is drawn as an OPEN
colonnade with pointer-events off, so it reads as an entrance without
occluding or blocking the room panels behind it. Rooms are panels on the
facade, sized from their real plan widths.

Geometry is SVG, not a WebGL camera, because these coordinates can be checked
by hand — the viewBox is derived from the vertex extents rather than guessed,
which is what the previous camera-framing attempts kept getting wrong.

components/house/FloorPlanKey: a key plan of the selected floor, rooms
numbered rather than labelled in place (a name like "Spa Bath En-Suite"
cannot fit a 23-unit room without colliding with its neighbour) with the
names carried in the schedule beneath. Hovering a room in the drawing, the
plan or the schedule lights it in all three.

VillaSection: two columns — drawing left, plan + schedule + the compositions
that floor is usually specified in on the right. The standalone <FloorPlans />
section is dropped from /rooms, since it repeated the same four plans one
screen further down; the component itself is kept.

Catalogue sub-type chips move from a wrapping row, which stretched cards to
uneven heights and broke the grid's alignment, to a single-line scroll strip.
